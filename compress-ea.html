01 · The problem with most robots

“20 pips” means nothing
across symbols.

A stop measured in fixed pips or points is a different meaning on every chart — because a “point” is a different size on each instrument. Tune it on one symbol and you've silently re‑tuned it for the next. Below: the same 300 points, seen as a share of each symbol's price.

Same number, wildly different meaning → this is how overfitting sneaks in.

02 · The core idea

Expansion → compression → breakout.

Volatility contracts, energy coils, price bursts out. The EA doesn't predict the direction — it arms a stop order at the edge and lets price pull the trigger.

expansion · high ATR
03 · How it works

A 4‑step engine.

1 · Detect

Normalize ATR to basis‑points of price: (ATR/close)×10⁴. A bar is “compressed” when that value drops below the threshold.

2 · Confirm

Count consecutive compressed bars. After K of them — and only if the range stays tight — the squeeze is confirmed.

3 · Arm

A higher‑timeframe moving average picks the side. Arm a BUY STOP above the high, or a SELL STOP below the low.

4 · Manage

The moment it fills, the squeeze locks — one coil, one trade. An optional break‑even later trails the stop to entry to protect the position.

05 · The one big idea

One threshold,
every price scale.

Set the squeeze threshold once, in basis‑points. It auto‑scales into the right absolute ATR on any instrument. Drag it — watch four very different markets stay in sync.

If you'd hard‑coded any single absolute number instead, it would fit one row and break the other three.

07 · How to tune it

Hunt the plateau,
not the peak.

Because the EA is normalized, optimizing isn't about the prettiest curve — that's the overfit trap. A good setting sits inside a broad region of similarly‑good results and survives on a second symbol. Hover the map: a lone bright cell is a trap; a green plateau is real.
