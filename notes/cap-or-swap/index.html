If the war ends, the swap calls. If it doesn't, the fuel bill does.

Airlines are stopping their hedges. Lock in 2027 now at $3.51 a gallon, and peace could leave you $1.44 above the market with a $167m margin call. Stay open, and the next spike is all yours. A cap struck at the swap rate settles it: each month you pay the lower of the market and $3.51, plus one fee fixed today, about 52 cents a gallon. It is the price of deciding after you know.

Why it worksAbove $3.51 the policy pays the difference, so no month costs more than $3.51. Below it you buy at the market. Either way you get the better of the swap and no hedge, less the fee. Nobody has to forecast the war.

Replayed since 2008Struck at each year's swap rate, the same cap averaged $2.15 a gallon, against $2.11 for the swap and $2.30 with no hedge. Its worst year left 33 cents of regret. The swap's left 82 cents, no hedge's $1.61.

How firm is the feeNobody publishes jet option prices, so volatility is built from heating oil and crude's OVX index. With it 20% lower or 25% higher the fee is 42 cents to 64 cents. Sized to the errors forwards have really made, 84 cents. A seller adds its margin.

The margin call doesn't vanish. It shrinks, and it turns around.

A policy takes the margin call away from the airline. If the desk carrying the risk hedges it in futures, the call comes back far smaller and from the other side. The airline's came when prices fell. The desk's biggest comes when they spike, and its own hedge gains pay for most of it. Here is a cap struck 10% above the swap, sold each December and replayed day by day.
