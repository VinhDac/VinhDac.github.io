A validation note · option market-making

The vega penalty protects the close, not the half day

Lucic and Tse's model for an options book (Risk.net, 2025) says its risk controls make the strategy resilient to moves of the implied volatility surface. Its own example holds the surface still. Here it moves.

Dac Vinh Nguyen · 20 November 2026 · code and checks · the note as a PDF
implied vol against strike, 90% to 110% of spot, at 3 months, 6 months, 1 year and 3 years
  1. The example reproduces. The baseline's mean half-day value is 166.6 against about 167 read off the paper's Figure 1(b), and all 12 shapes the figure shows hold.
  2. The surface's moves add risk the example does not show. With each maturity moving by its own loading on the VIX, the standard deviation of the half day's value goes from 4.80 to 5.45; if every maturity moved as far as the VIX, to 9.34.
  3. The example's penalty works at the close. Ten times the terminal penalty cuts the risk the closing book carries into the next half day by 21% (60% if every maturity moved alike), and the risk the move brings during the session by 4%. A hundred times cuts the latter by 4%.
  4. The running penalty does the in-session work. The paper defines it; the example sets it to zero. On the vega the move loads on, it cuts the in-session risk by 51% for 0.14 of mean value, where ten times the terminal penalty costs 0.79.
  5. The closed form's approximation holds at the half day. Against the exact HJB (6), solved for one option at a time, the closed form's quotes give up at most 0.007% of the value over the half day and 0.014% over five days.

1 · the half day, drawnWhere the book holds its vega

The ribbon is the book's percentage-vega through the session, its root mean square over 8,000 paths on a still surface, drawn above and below a line. The paper's penalty, α, is charged on the book at the close; a running penalty, β, is charged all session. Pick one and compare it with the paper's, the grey outline.

Ten times the terminal penalty keeps the book's vega within 4% of the baseline's for the first seven tenths of the session, then pulls it to 1.1 at the close, where the baseline holds 3.5: the inventory term of the quotes, Θ2, is −A at the close and fades as the close recedes. A running penalty holds Θ2 near a level of its own however far the close is, so it leans on the book all day. Its price is written r/T: at r = α it charges a session of holding a vega what α charges for holding it past the close.

2 · cost against riskWhat each penalty buys

The cost of a penalty is the mean value it gives up on a still surface, path by path against the baseline; the fills and the spot are the same for every penalty. What it buys is the in-session risk it takes away: the spread of what the surface's move did to its own trades. Each line is one kind of penalty at growing strength.

The factor matters as much as the strength. The paper's buckets penalise the at-the-money buckets lightly, where most of the vega sits. On the vega the move loads on, each option's percentage-vega times its maturity's loading, 0.2/T costs 0.14; on the book's whole vega the same price costs 0.74, because it also leans on long-dated vega the move barely touches.

3 · how the surface movesShort maturities move most

Through the half day the surface shifts along a Brownian bridge to a day's change of the VIX scaled to half a day: 9,319 daily changes from 2 January 1990 to 18 November 2026, a half-day standard deviation of 1.18 vol points. On the same 4,769 days, the VIX3M moved 0.63 of a point for each point of the VIX (correlation 0.95), between 0.60 and 0.66 in three separate stretches of those years. A power law through 30 and 93 days gives the book's maturities these loadings; past 93 days it is an assumption.

5 · the closed formIts approximation, checked

The quotes (12) rest on a second-order expansion of the exponentials in the HJB equation (6); the paper's footnote calls them very satisfactory over a short horizon. For one option at a time, with gamma frozen as (20) freezes it, I solved (6) exactly on an inventory grid wide enough that doubling it changes nothing, valued the closed form's quotes by the same equation's linear twin, and set the two side by side at the paper's parameters. Where the book actually is at the close, the first to ninety-ninth percentile of its inventory, the quotes differ by at most 1.3% of 1/κ, the spread's own scale.

6 · before productionWhat I would test next

  • Order flow that reacts to the surface's moves, and spot and vol that move together; here both are independent of the move.
  • The surface the paper used and loadings past three months read from longer indices, in place of a figure read by eye and a power law.
  • The running penalty's factor and price chosen from the desk's own P&L attribution: the factor the surface actually moves on, priced so that the in-session risk it leaves is the risk the desk means to run.

The paper: author manuscript at UCL Discovery. Data: FRED, series VIXCLS and VXVCLS (Cboe). 40 checks of the code hold, and all 69 faults planted in it on purpose, one at a time, are caught.
