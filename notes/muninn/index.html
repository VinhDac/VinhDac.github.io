Your posting says agents make it cheap to test thousands of ideas, and so cheap to overfit. I let a search fly 4,536 macro ideas over 8.5 years. The best, the yen leading silver, had a Sharpe of 1.20, then −0.07 over the next 9.9 years. A ledger of every flight could tell before the market did.

I · Huginn's best flight

The winner looked like a discovery.

Huginn is the search. It flew seven families of macro signals over fifteen ETFs in equities, bonds, commodities and currencies: trend, moving-average crosses, reversals, cross-sectional momentum, trend switched by a regime, one market leading another, and a macro indicator timing a market. Each family came in several look-backs, asset groups, volatility settings and rebalancing speeds: 4,728 flights, 4,536 of them in the market often enough to judge.

Muninn is the ledger. It keeps every flight and flies it again on 200 calendars rotated by a year or more. There a signal keeps its habits, its costs and its likeness to every other flight, but it cannot know the next day. The best flight a whole search finds on such a calendar is the bar.

Fly a night where nothing can be predicted

Each night is one rotated calendar. The whole search flies again, and the best flight lands on the line. The amber mark is the real winner.

Of all 200 nights, 44 found a better best flight than the real winner.
II · The leaderboard is a census

The top goes to the family that flew most.

Rank the flights by Sharpe and the top belongs to whichever family flew most, not the one that knew most. One market leading another was 1,260 of the 4,536 flights. Its average Sharpe was 0.01 in sample and −0.06 after, yet it took 28 of the top 45 places. On the calendars where nothing can be predicted it supplied the best flight 96 times in 200. Many loosely related flights are many lottery tickets.

III · What survives is the flock

Judge the family, not the bird.

A flight's rotated calendars keep everything about it except timing, so its Sharpe minus theirs is the part only timing explains. Averaged over a family, that margin held. The four families with +0.14 to +0.17 of timing in sample all earned after 2016, 0.06 to 0.26. The three with +0.04 or less all lost, −0.32 to −0.06.

It holds when the split moves and when costs double. In every version the best single flight failed afterwards, at −0.10 to 0.08, and luck beat it on 16% to 59% of calendars. Family timing and later Sharpe kept their order, with rank correlation 0.71 to 0.82.

IV · Muninn in the agent loop

Not a formula. A habit.

The agent writes down every flight, and the ledger flies them again where nothing can be predicted.

from muninn import Ledger

book = Ledger(returns, split="2016-12-31")      # 200 rotated calendars
for idea in agent.propose():                    # Huginn: every idea, kept or not
    book.record(idea.name, idea.family, idea.weights)

book.p_value(1.20)    # 0.22: luck finds a better best on 22% of calendars
book.bar(0.95)        # 1.33: the best flight luck finds, 95th percentile
book.families()       # each family's Sharpe minus its own rotated calendars
  1. Record every flight, the failures too. The bar is set by all of them, and an agent that adapts to what it has seen hides most of its flights unless something writes them down.
  2. Measure the bar, don't assume it. Counted three ways, the same 4,536 flights are 26, 799 or 889 independent tries, and the bar moves from 0.69 to 1.10. The rotated calendars need no count: they already know how alike the flights are. Here they took 39 seconds on a laptop.
  3. Promote families, not winners. A family's margin over its own rotated calendars is the part of its Sharpe that is timing.
  4. Track forward what was promoted, against the bar of the search that found it.

V · Receipts

Data. Fifteen ETFs (SPY, EFA, EEM, EWJ, TLT, IEF, LQD, HYG, GLD, SLV, DBC, USO, UUP, FXE, FXY) and three indicators (the VIX, the 10-year yield and the 3-month bill), daily adjusted closes from Yahoo, 11 Apr 2007 to 18 Nov 2026. In sample 18 Jun 2008 to 30 Dec 2016 (8.5 years); after, 3 Jan 2017 to 18 Nov 2026 (9.9 years). Positions are set at a close and held the next day, at 3 basis points per unit of turnover.

What it is not. A grid of 4,728 flights stands in for an agent. A real agent adapts to what it has seen, which hides its flights and makes the ledger matter more. There is no carry or value, which need futures curves, and ETFs stand in for futures. The probability of backtest overfitting for the whole search, by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation over 16 blocks, is 0.45.

Checks. 14 rules the study depends on, each seen to fail: 15 planted faults, from a signal that reads tomorrow's price to calendars that are never rotated, were all caught. Every number on this page is read from results.json, which study.py writes.
