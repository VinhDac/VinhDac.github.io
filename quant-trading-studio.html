Warm-up · the core problem

Which trader actually has an edge?

Both finished the year up the same amount. Only one did it with a repeatable method. Look at the shape, then choose.

Luck hides in the shape: profit from one lucky move looks identical to skill on a scorecard. The whole studio exists to tell them apart.

Test 1 · read the market

Name the market's mood

Before optimizing anything, the system describes the market with two honest axes: memory and volatility. Together they make six regimes, R1–R6. Try it on a real-looking sample.

Six regimes let you split time honestly — build a strategy in one regime, prove it on a different occurrence of the same one. Never a random date split (neighbouring days move together, so random leaks the future into the past).

Test 2 · the overfit trap

One idea becomes thousands of settings

A single strategy has hundreds of parameter combinations. Below are 160 of them, plotted by profit on the data we optimized on. Click the one you'd trade — then test it on data it has never seen.

Pick a dot ↑

The tallest peaks are usually overfit — they win once and collapse. So the system never picks max profit: it clusters the settings, keeps a few genuinely different survivors, and re-tests each on unseen regimes (PASS / DEGRADE / FAIL).

The honest ending

As a startup, it failed.

The portfolio was stable — but the real drawdown ran ~30% worse than the model, and the strategy I ranked second quietly made the money. I ran out of capital and time to keep proving skill from luck.

A clean model isn't the one that makes the most money.
It's the one you can prove is right in the shortest time.
