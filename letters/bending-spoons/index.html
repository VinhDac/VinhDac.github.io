Yahoo's data says a FTSE 100 fund returned 4.2% a year. It returned 8.2%.

Dear Bending Spoons team,

I found that gap while building a small market-data warehouse. For London shares quoted in pence, Yahoo applies each dividend at exactly a hundredth of its size, as if the pence were pounds. It did so on 838 of 840 dividends, and on none of 1,970 in New York. Nothing breaks; the returns just come out quietly wrong.

I forecast Britain's electricity demand half-hour by half-hour over 971 test days. Gradient boosting missed by 3.2% on average, a typical error of 794 MW on a system averaging 26 GW. The LSTM tied on the validation year, then fell behind, furthest when solar was hiding the most demand from the grid. I would love to blame the solar, but the data cannot tell it apart from the model simply getting older, so my write-up says exactly that.
