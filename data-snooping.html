1 · What deep learning really is, and the plan

We have a job: put a label on the next case. What do we build?

Almost always the same thing: a small neural network. A few features go in, get mixed and bent through a hidden layer, and come out as a guess.

1 · What deep learning really is, and the plan

How does it learn? Not by us programming it, but by correcting itself. The same four small moves, over and over.

1Forwardtake the numbers in, mix them into a guess
2Lossscore how wrong the guess is, in one number
3Gradientwhich way to nudge each knob, downhill
4Updatetake one small step that way

A few thousand rounds of those four, and the guesses come out mostly right.

1 · What deep learning really is, and the plan

One small marvel hides in those four moves.

Score the wrongness this way, and the correction each step makes is exactly how far off the guess was, no more.

5 · The thing worth keeping

That enemy has a name. The books call it data snooping, and the definition you will find is narrow: you try a great many models and keep the one that scored best, and it scored best only by luck. The winner's curse.

That is real, and we met it head on. But almost nothing that actually fooled us was “trying too many models.” The leak was a quiet assumption about cutting the data. The lie was the wrong ruler on lopsided classes. The ghost was one wild day and a broken test.

5 · The thing worth keeping

So here is the definition we actually earned. Snooping is the plain, everyday result of trusting a number without checking what each step quietly took for granted.

It is not the exception.
It is the default.

The textbook names one corner of the room. What we found was the whole room. A number can be bent by a leak, the wrong measure, one bad day, a broken test, secret overlap, or too much searching, and from the outside, you cannot tell any of them from an honest one.

5 · The thing worth keeping

Which is why the thing worth keeping was never a result. It was a way of working, and it is simpler than it sounds.

1Name the bet. Before you run a step, say out loud what it assumes, and ask the data in front of you, not the textbook, whether that is true here.
2Clean before you reach for the tool. A powerful tool let loose on a messy problem will find the mess and proudly call it a discovery.
3Test on ground it has never seen. A real pattern travels to a new place; a fake one dies on the road.
4Seal one test. Open it exactly once, at the very end, and never let it help you choose.
5Know the ceiling. Work out beforehand how good an honest answer could be, so a number sailing past it makes you suspicious, not pleased.
5 · The thing worth keeping

And underneath all of it, the quietest lesson, the one that took three chapters to feel. Do not try to be right the first time. You will not be, and you do not need to be.

Build yourself a loop that catches you when you are wrong, and then trust the loop instead of trusting yourself. That, in the end, was where the calm came from. Not from being clever, but from having a way of working we could lean on even as we kept getting things wrong.
